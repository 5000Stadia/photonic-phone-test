Photonic Lab

The people of the holodeck, live in your browser. Every person here is made from a few words and a seed. The bodies are MakeHuman's (CC0), each walking on their own skeleton; about one in five wears a gentle 1660 outfit (women and girls the gentlewoman's silk bodice and skirt over a padded roll, men and boys of six and up the gentleman's short doublet, divided petticoat breeches with ribbon at the knee, and stockings) and three in five a working one (women and girls a laced waistcoat, neckerchief, apron and ankle-length skirt; men and boys a doublet with skirts to the hip, close breeches with a knee band, and stockings), in drab, grey, blue, green, madder and weld; the rest still wear a plain base layer in placeholder colours.

Waiting for your eye

Lineup: thirty people from their words live
Thirty people from a baby to ninety, in three rows, of every build, each made from a few words ("a girl of 8 in a red dress", "a stout gentleman in black", "an old washerwoman in an apron") and dressed for 1660 from the same words: rank, garment and colour. Each is drawn at the near tier; stand them or walk them in place. A colour an outfit has no dye for is said under the caption, and the five worst cases of the clothing's clipping check are among them. Boys under six and babies wear the base layer only, as built.
Judge: does each person look like their words and their age, and does the dress fit their rank?
Two walks side by side live
Our fitted walk (left) and a raw motion-capture walk (right, 100STYLE Neutral), on the same plain figure drawn only from joint positions, same height, walking in place. Side view or three-quarter front, at full or half speed.
Judge: does the captured walk on the right look alive where ours looks stiff? If both look stiff, the body is the problem, not the walk data.
Hairstyle lineup live
Seven people at conversation distance (about 2 m), each a different age, sex and skin, in clothes: six ready-made hairstyles (a bob, long layered, a short crop, a side braid, an afro, an updo) fitted to their heads, and today's grown hair for comparison. Step through them, turn them (front, three-quarter, side, back, or a slow turn), pull in to the head, and try five hair colours.
Judge: against Baldur's Gate 3 and Hogwarts Legacy: do the strands read as locks, is the silhouette soft, is there a dark root and a lighter tip, is the highlight a clean band along the hair, and does the back look as good as the front? Which direction should we build on?
Meeting head-on live
Nine people meet head-on in a lane, seen from above, and one crosses them. They steer round each other early.
Judge: do they start to swerve early enough, and does a swerve look like a person's?
300 in a plaza live
300 people in a 30 m square, each walking to one spot and then another. They steer round each other; when two touch anyway, they are nudged apart and slow for a moment. Four in five are dressed for 1660: about one in five as gentlefolk and three in five as working folk (the rest still in the plain base layer).
Judge: does a brush read as a bump or a slide? Anyone stalling where a real person would just go?
Phone benchmarks run on your iPhone
Everything in one run, about 4 minutes: first the real people (this plaza of 300, with and without the 1660 outfits, on WebGPU and on WebGL2 side by side), then the stand-in crowd (300 and 1000) and the near person, each on both, and Safari's same-200-people check.
Do: in Safari, set Auto-Lock to Never, tap "Run every benchmark", leave the phone on the table. At the end tap "Send results".
